\section{Six Models, One Task}

This document compares the six robot+object world models that live
side-by-side under \texttt{scripts/world\_model/Physics/Robot\_and\_Object}.
Every model predicts the same 31-dimensional state $s_t = (q,
\dot q, p^o, r^o, v^o, \omega^o)$ on the Franka Lift task, takes the
same recorded torque $\tau_t \in \mathbb{R}^{9}$ as input, and is
evaluated against the same heavy / light cube datasets.  The models
differ in
\begin{enumerate}
  \item how they encode (or whether they encode) a per-episode
    physical context;
  \item how the robot ODE is structured;
  \item how the object integrator is structured (which is where the
    six models differ the most);
  \item which losses receive an explicit training signal.
\end{enumerate}
The folders are:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lp{0.65\linewidth}}
\toprule
Folder & One-line summary \\
\midrule
\texttt{GT\_dynamics/}             & DeLaN robot ODE + an MLP object-acceleration head; the GT physical context $\xi$ is fed in directly. \\
\texttt{Context\_DeLaN/}           & Same head topology as \texttt{GT\_dynamics} but conditions on a learned latent $z$ from an LSTM/MLP history encoder. \\
\texttt{MCGDF/}                    & DeLaN robot ODE + structured contact wrench $F_c$; Newton--Euler integrator on the object side; variational context encoder with FiLM. \\
\texttt{No\_object\_Euler/}        & MCGDF robot side; kinematic-blend object side with a continuous coupling factor $\alpha_t \in [0,1]$; online latent-update filter. \\
\texttt{MCGDF\_object\_mlp/}       & MCGDF robot side; the object integrator is removed entirely and replaced by a 13-D next-state MLP with FiLM. \\
\texttt{Contact\_Flag\_discrete/}  & MCGDF robot side with the reaction torque gated; the object integrator is Newton--Euler gated by a discrete contact flag $c_t \in \{0,1\}$ via straight-through estimator. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\section{What All Six Share}

\paragraph{State vector.}
All six use the same 31-D state with the same ordering
\((q, \dot q, p^o, r^o, v^o, \omega^o)\), so checkpoints from different
models can be compared frame-by-frame and the
\texttt{compare\_models\_pred\_error\_*.py} scripts in this folder can
mix them.

\paragraph{Franka FK and the geometric Jacobian.}
The five MCGDF-lineage models reuse the same PyTorch
\texttt{FrankaForwardKinematics} module from
\texttt{MCGDF/models.py}; \texttt{Context\_DeLaN} and
\texttt{GT\_dynamics} have an equivalent numpy/torch chain.  These
are analytic, not learned.

\paragraph{Robot integration.}
All six use semi-implicit Euler for the robot kinematics
$\dot q_{t+1} = \dot q_t + \ddot q_t \Delta t$,
$q_{t+1} = q_t + \dot q_{t+1} \Delta t$ once the robot acceleration
$\ddot q_t$ is in hand.  The five physics variants
(\texttt{GT\_dynamics}, \texttt{Context\_DeLaN}, \texttt{MCGDF},
\texttt{No\_object\_Euler}, \texttt{Contact\_Flag\_discrete}) all
solve for $\ddot q_t$ through the same DeLaN-structured equation
\begin{equation}
  H(q, \cdot)\,\ddot q
  \;=\;
  \tau + r_\theta - c(q, \dot q, \cdot) - g(q, \cdot)
  - d\,\dot q - f\,\tanh(\dot q / \varepsilon_f) + \tau_{\mathrm{contact}}.
  \label{eq:cmp-robot}
\end{equation}
The differences enter through the gating on $\tau_{\mathrm{contact}}$
and whether the DeLaN heads are FiLM-modulated by $z$.

\paragraph{Quaternion update.}
All variants that integrate $\omega^o$ then use the closed-form
exponential map
$r^o_{t+1} = r^o_t \otimes \exp(\tfrac12 \omega^o_{t+1} \Delta t)$.
The lone exception is \texttt{MCGDF\_object\_mlp}, which emits the
quaternion slot of the next state directly from the MLP and
normalises it.

\section{Physics on the Robot Side}

The robot side is fairly uniform.  The major axes of variation are:
(i)~whether the DeLaN heads $H, c, g$ are conditioned on a latent
$z$ (FiLM); (ii)~which signal forms the contact reaction torque;
(iii)~whether that torque is always applied or gated.

\midrule

\midrule
\texttt{GT\_dynamics} &

    \(

    \).

    & The 6-D acceleration vector. \\

    & Continuous coupling factor $\alpha_t \in [0, 1]$. \\

    \(

    \)
    quaternion slot normalised.
    & The 13-D next-state vector itself. \\
\texttt{Contact\_Flag\_discrete} &
    Newton--Euler \emph{gated} by $c_t \in \{0, 1\}$:
    \(
      m_o \dot v^o = m_o g + c_t F_c^{\mathrm{lin}},
    \)
    \(
      I_w \dot \omega^o + \omega^o \times I_w \omega^o = c_t F_c^{\mathrm{ang}}.
    \)
    & Discrete flag $c_t$ + contact wrench $F_c$. \\

\section{Context Encoder Module}

\paragraph{Three families, six choices.}
\begin{longtable}{p{0.20\linewidth} p{0.20\linewidth} p{0.18\linewidth} p{0.30\linewidth}}
\toprule
Model & Encoder type & Latent prior & How $z$ flows through the dynamics \\
\midrule
\texttt{GT\_dynamics}            & none ($\xi$ used directly)     & ---                & $\xi$ concatenated into the MLP inputs. \\
\texttt{Context\_DeLaN}          & LSTM (default) or MLP          & deterministic      & $z$ FiLMs the DeLaN block + concatenated into residual/object MLPs. \\
\texttt{MCGDF}                   & Variational (VAE)              & $\mathcal{N}(0, I)$ KL                & $z$ optionally FiLMs DeLaN; FiLMs $r_\theta$ and $F_c$ heads. \\
\texttt{No\_object\_Euler}       & Variational (VAE) + \emph{online filter} & $\mathcal{N}(0, I)$ KL                & Same as MCGDF, plus $z$ updates online during the filter window via \texttt{LatentInnovationUpdate}. \\
\texttt{MCGDF\_object\_mlp}      & Variational (VAE)              & $\mathcal{N}(0, I)$ KL                & Same as MCGDF; the new \texttt{ObjectStateMLP} is also FiLMed by $z$. \\
\texttt{Contact\_Flag\_discrete} & Variational (VAE) + online filter (inherited from No-Object-Euler) & $\mathcal{N}(0, I)$ KL                & Same as No\_object\_Euler. \\
\bottomrule
\caption{Context encoder architectures.  ``deterministic'' means the
encoder emits $z$ directly (no $\mu, \log\sigma^2$); ``variational''
means $z \sim q_\phi(z \mid \mathrm{history})$ with a reparameterised
sample at training and the mean at deployment.}
\label{tab:cmp-encoder}
\end{longtable}

\paragraph{Constant vs.\ time-evolving $z$.}
In four of the six models ($\texttt{GT\_dynamics}$, $\texttt{Context\_DeLaN}$,
$\texttt{MCGDF}$, $\texttt{MCGDF\_object\_mlp}$) the latent (or oracle
context) is encoded \emph{once} at the start of the rollout and held
\emph{constant} for the whole horizon $H$.  In two
($\texttt{No\_object\_Euler}$ and $\texttt{Contact\_Flag\_discrete}$),
the encoder still produces $z_0$ once, but the optional
\texttt{LatentInnovationUpdate} cell refines $z$ step-by-step
\(
  z_t = z_{t-1} + \sigma(\text{gate}) \odot \tanh(\delta)
\)
using the per-step object-state prediction residual as evidence (a
Kalman-innovation analog).  This refinement runs inside the closed-
loop filter window of length \texttt{--filter\_steps}, before the
open-loop rollout.

\section{Losses}

\paragraph{The shared menu.}
Three loss families are present in every model:
(i)~\textbf{rollout MSE} on the $H$-step predicted trajectory (with
per-component weights and a sign-invariant quaternion MSE);
(ii)~\textbf{supervised object dynamics} -- MSEs of
$\dot v^o, \dot \omega^o$ against the recorded
\texttt{root\_lin\_acc\_w} / \texttt{root\_ang\_acc\_w};
(iii)~\textbf{supervised robot dynamics} -- MSEs of $H, c, g, \ddot q$
and the inverse-dynamics torque against the dataset's recorded
fields.

\paragraph{Differences.}
\begin{longtable}{p{0.22\linewidth} p{0.74\linewidth}}
\toprule
Loss / regularizer & Where it lives \\
\midrule
$\lambda_{\mathrm{contact}} \|F_c\|^2$ regularizer       & Only in the four models that predict $F_c$: \texttt{MCGDF}, \texttt{No\_object\_Euler}, \texttt{MCGDF\_object\_mlp}, \texttt{Contact\_Flag\_discrete}.  Keeps $F_c$ near zero on no-contact frames. \\
Pre-contact baseline subtraction on the residual target & \texttt{MCGDF}, \texttt{No\_object\_Euler}, \texttt{MCGDF\_object\_mlp}, \texttt{Contact\_Flag\_discrete}.  Approach~A of mismatch~\#2 in the MCGDF description; not present in \texttt{GT\_dynamics} or \texttt{Context\_DeLaN}. \\
KL$\bigl(q_\phi(z \mid h) \,\|\, \mathcal{N}(0, I)\bigr)$ & The four variational models: \texttt{MCGDF}, \texttt{No\_object\_Euler}, \texttt{MCGDF\_object\_mlp}, \texttt{Contact\_Flag\_discrete}.  Not in \texttt{GT\_dynamics} (no encoder) or \texttt{Context\_DeLaN} (deterministic $z$). \\
Soft-weighted InfoNCE on $z$            & Same four variational models.  Anchors $z$ around $\xi$-distance in the batch. \\
Context regression head $\hat \xi(z)$ MSE & Optional in the four variational models; default $\lambda = 0$. \\
Damping / friction supervised loss      & All five physics variants (when those scalars are learnable).  Not in \texttt{Context\_DeLaN} (no damping head). \\
\bottomrule
\caption{Loss / regularizer differences.  The rollout MSE and
supervised dynamics targets are shared by all six and not listed.}
\label{tab:cmp-loss}
\end{longtable}

\paragraph{One subtlety on the object-acceleration loss.}
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{objectGT}}$ is computed identically in all six
models -- it MSEs \texttt{aux["object\_lin\_acc"]} and
\texttt{aux["object\_ang\_acc"]} against the recorded
\texttt{root\_lin\_acc\_w} / \texttt{root\_ang\_acc\_w}.  Its
\emph{meaning} differs, though:
\begin{itemize}
  \item In \texttt{GT\_dynamics}, \texttt{Context\_DeLaN},
    \texttt{MCGDF}, \texttt{Contact\_Flag\_discrete}: the aux fields
    are the integrator's actual accelerations, so the loss is a
    direct constraint on the network's acceleration prediction.
  \item In \texttt{No\_object\_Euler} and
    \texttt{MCGDF\_object\_mlp}: the aux fields are
    finite-difference diagnostics computed from predicted
    velocities, so the loss is an \emph{indirect} constraint that
    pulls the predicted velocity change toward the recorded
    acceleration.
\end{itemize}

\section{Practical Implications}

\paragraph{When each model is the natural baseline.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{GT\_dynamics} as the \emph{oracle baseline}:
    everything below it on the prior axis should be compared against
    its accuracy as an upper-bound.  When MCGDF beats GT\_dynamics on
    the rollout MSE that is informative -- it means the explicit
    Newton--Euler integrator pays for itself.
  \item \texttt{Context\_DeLaN} as the \emph{no-oracle baseline}:
    the deterministic-$z$ equivalent of GT\_dynamics that does not
    consume $\xi$ at deployment.
  \item \texttt{MCGDF} as the \emph{maximally-physical baseline}: if
    a contact-rich variant beats MCGDF on cube prediction it points
    to a model component that exists outside the rigid-body
    integrator (slip, soft fingers, off-COM contact).
  \item \texttt{No\_object\_Euler} as the \emph{soft-coupling
    baseline}: tests whether a continuous coupling factor is enough
    to capture the carry/release dichotomy.
  \item \texttt{Contact\_Flag\_discrete} as the \emph{discrete-mode
    baseline}: tests whether a hard mode switch beats the soft one.
  \item \texttt{MCGDF\_object\_mlp} as the \emph{capacity baseline}:
    upper-bound MLP capacity for the next-state prediction with no
    structural prior.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Which axis is most important to ablate?}
For the Lift task in this codebase the empirical answer (from the
cross-model error scripts in this folder) seems to be the
\emph{object-side inductive bias}, not the encoder family.  Switching
between the LSTM, MLP, and variational encoders shifts the rollout
MSE by single-digit percent; switching between Newton--Euler, hard
flag, soft $\alpha$, and pure MLP shifts it more.
